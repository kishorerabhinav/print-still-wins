E-books still no match for printed books

In every one of ten major book markets, more people bought a printed book than an e-book in 2020. The gap is widest in Western Europe and narrowest in East Asia.

01 · The gap

Print leads everywhere, by very different margins

The e-reader was supposed to replace the paperback. More than a decade after its launch, it has not. In all ten markets surveyed, more people bought a printed book than an e-book.

The size of that lead varies a lot. In Germany, 58% of people bought a printed book and only 10.4% bought an e-book, a gap of nearly 48 percentage points. In China, the gap is under 8 points.

02 · Who's switching

China and the US come closest to parity

Percentage-point gaps hide one thing: markets where fewer people buy books will always show smaller gaps. India's gap looks modest only because few people there buy books in either format.

A fairer comparison is how many e-book buyers there are for every ten print buyers. On that measure, China stands apart at nearly eight. The United States and South Korea follow at about five. France and Germany sit below two.

Plotting both shares together shows how far each market sits from parity. Each line from the corner is a fixed ratio; the steeper a market sits, the closer it is to parity.

03 · Why print holds

Readers still want the feel of paper

There are several explanations for print's staying power. One study suggests readers retain information better from a printed page than from a screen. Consumer surveys also find that e-books have not reproduced the physical experience of a book: the weight, the paper, and the sense of progress as pages build up on one side.

Researchers call this haptic dissonance. When a new product removes a sensory cue that people value, they hold back from adopting it, even when it is cheaper or more convenient. Gerlach and Buxmann documented this effect in e-book adoption in 2011.

04 · Outlook

The digital market is still growing

E-books are unlikely to replace print, but they do not need to. The digital book market is still expected to grow, even as print keeps its lead in every market surveyed.
